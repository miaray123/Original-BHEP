The Black Hair Exposome Framework

A life-course lens for understanding Black hair, cumulative exposure and the systems that shape health.

What is the Black Hair Exposome?

The exposome describes the environmental exposures and conditions people experience across their lives. BHEP applies this lens to Black hair care, starting with culture rather than treating Black hair as a risk factor. It considers how products, practices, places and structural conditions intersect from childhood through adulthood.

Hair care is learned through family, community, salons and barbershops. Products may be used repeatedly, in combination and over decades. Exposure can occur through skin contact or inhalation depending on the product and practice. These experiences coexist with exposures outside the hair-care routine. The framework asks what happens when these burdens overlap, and who has the power to change them.

Six interconnected dimensions

Culture & identity

Family traditions, hair memories, self-expression, resistance, belonging and racialized beauty standards shape how and why people care for their hair.

Routine & products

Age at first use, frequency, duration, combinations of products, styling practices and ingredient disclosure shape opportunities for exposure across the life course.

Economics & access

Affordability, availability, marketing, time, salon access and understandable ingredient information influence choices. Safer care must be accessible, not a luxury.

Internal & external environments

Indoor exposures in homes, salons and barbershops include ventilation, fragrances, aerosols and cleaning products. External environments include traffic pollution, industrial emissions, wildfire smoke, neighborhood conditions and other environmental burdens. Hair-related exposures coexist with these conditions; they are not separate from environmental justice.

Health across the life course

Consider exposure pathways, timing, cumulative burdens, occupational exposure among stylists and barbers, and evidence concerning respiratory, skin, reproductive and other health outcomes. Evidence varies by chemical and outcome; exposure does not establish disease.

Policy & regulation

Ingredient transparency, product safety standards, enforcement, workplace protections and discrimination shape the conditions under which Black people care for their hair.

Why cumulative exposure matters

A child’s first hair routine, an adult’s repeated product use, a stylist’s occupational exposure and the air quality of the neighborhood are different pieces of a larger picture. BHEP seeks to understand these connections without assuming every product or exposure produces the same health effect.

Our approach centers Black people of all ages and genders and recognizes that individual choices alone cannot correct structural inequities.
